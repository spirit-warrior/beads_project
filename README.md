# beads_project

## Intro

We have a large fiberboard plank which was used for about 27 years as the sitting surface for Amma's chair. When Amma's chair was modified in 2024, this piece was left over. This project is about creating beads from this plank which is made of compressed wood fibers.

In the end, the beads will be added to necklaces and bracelets to be sold to support Amma's charities.

Fiberboard is not a highly durable material and so the beads need to be treated so they don't break or dissolve over time. The beads should be highly water resistant and wear resistant so they will not deteriorate even after many decades. The interior of the hole that is drilled through the bead should also be hardened and protected.

All materials and treatments should be non-toxic, food-safe, and otherwise eco-friendly. After applying the protective layers, which are essential for durability, the surface of the beads will be hard and glossy, nothing like the surface texture of untreated wood beads.

When cutting and shaping the material into beads, we definitely want to save and utilize the sawdust.

# Preferred Method

- Overview

  - Stabilize the fiberboard using PC-Petrifier

  - Apply topcoat of epoxy resin (or polyurehane) for durability and waterproofing

  - Apply each product by dipping the beads. Painting and other methods are impractical for large numbers (hundreds) of beads

  - Meets the goals for durability, non-toxicity, and easy of use

## 1 - Initial preparation

- The thickness of the fiberboard is greater than the optimal diameter for beads. Therefore split the fiberboard into thinner sheets before cutting it.

- Use a scroll saw or fine-tooth jigsaw to cut small bead shapes from the fiberboard.
- Drill the holes for stringing
- Size of the drilled hole should be large enough so that it doesn't become clogged during the dipping processes.
- Sand the beads to smooth any rough edges.

## 2 - Application of Hardener - PC-Petrifier

- Dipping

  - Submerge the beads in PC-Petrifier. Let them soak briefly to allow the product to fully penetrate the fiberboard.

  - Place the beads on a wire rack, skewer, or fishing line to drip dry.

  - Rotate periodically to prevent sticking or pooling.

- Curing

  - Allow the beads to dry and cure fully. This may take a few hours to overnight, depending on humidity and temperature.

  - Once cured, the beads should feel significantly harder and more durable.

## 3 - Application of Topcoat - ArtResin or Polyurethane

After hardening with PC-Petrifier, next step is to apply a topcoat to enhance durability, water resistance, and aesthetics. ArtResin is preferred but water-based polyurethane is also a good option.

- About ArtResin Epoxy Resin

  - Creates a high-gloss, highly durable finish
  - Waterproof
  - Maximum wear-resistant
  - Certified non-toxic
  - More labor involved

#### Application of ArtResin

- Work in a well-ventilated area
- String a manageable number of beads onto a length of fishing line
- Submerge the beads and fishing line in the resin
- Hang the beads and fishing line to dry
- Use a heat gun or small torch to remove bubbles from the resin surface.
- Rotate the beads periodically to ensure a more even coat.
- Try to prevent the fishing line from becoming irreversibly stuck to the beads

<hr>

#### Alternative - Water-based Polyurethane

- Dries faster
- Non-toxic when cured
- Less forgiving about moisture
- Apply 2 or 3 coats with drying time between
- Example products
  - General Finishes High-Performance Topcoat
  - Minwax Polycrylic

# Further Discussion

## 1. "Cut and Coat" Beads

- Cutting Beads

Use a scroll saw or fine-tooth jigsaw to cut small bead shapes from the fiberboard. Sand them down to smooth the edges.

- Coating with Polyurethane

Apply multiple layers of water-based polyurethane or a similar non-toxic, clear, durable coating. Ensure the product is certified for prolonged skin contact (look for "food-safe" or "skin-safe" certifications).

- Interior Coating

Use a fine brush or pipe cleaner to coat the drilled hole to prevent moisture absorption.

- Drying

Allow the beads to cure fully between coats to ensure maximum durability.

## 2. Sawdust Beads

- Sawdust Consolidation

Mix sawdust with a strong, non-toxic binder like eco-friendly resin or wood glue (e.g., Titebond III, which is water-resistant and non-toxic).

- Molding

Shape the mixture into beads using silicone molds or by hand. Allow them to harden completely before sanding and coating.

- Coating

After the beads have hardened, apply the same durable, non-toxic coating.

## 3. Soaking or Impregnation

- Hardeners

Soak the fiberboard beads in a water-based wood stabilizer (e.g., Minwax Wood Hardener). This can help consolidate and waterproof the material from within.

- Acrylic Resin

You can also dip the beads in a thin layer of food-safe acrylic resin to achieve greater durability.

- Process

After soaking, allow the beads to dry thoroughly and apply an outer protective coating.

## Premium Methods

## 1. Vacuum Stabilization

- Process

Place the beads in a vacuum chamber with a non-toxic stabilizing resin (e.g., Cactus Juice). The vacuum removes air from the porous fiberboard and allows the resin to fully penetrate the material.

- Curing

Heat-cure the beads after stabilization to harden them.

- Outcome

Extremely hard, durable, and water-resistant beads.

## 2. Using UV-Curable Resins

- Resin Application

Dip or coat the beads in UV-curable resin used in high-quality jewelry-making. These resins are often non-toxic and highly durable once cured.

- Curing

Use a UV light source to harden the resin within seconds, ensuring even coating and minimal environmental impact.

## 3. Sawdust Composite with Bio-Resin

- Binder

Use a bio-based resin like EcoPoxy to mix with the sawdust. This creates a sustainable, eco-friendly bead material that can be shaped and polished.

- Molding

Cast the mixture into intricate bead molds for unique designs.

- Durability

Bio-resins offer excellent wear resistance and are non-toxic.
